# Generated charts

`generateWorld(players, seed)` accepts 2–10 unique catalog factions and a uint32 seed, including zero. The participant list identifies the host independently of site identifiers. Public site IDs, names, and array order are shuffled independently of starting roles so distant labels do not reveal hidden cores or ownership. The result contains rocks and square bounds. New charts use the `balanced.v2` generator with `torus` topology; the caller records that metadata in world rules. Existing saved coordinates are not regenerated or migrated. It doesn't mutate participants or retain random state between calls. Its local integer sampler is independent of recruitment and engine randomness. Integration must keep the seed private rather than adding it to a public GameView.

Each participant owns five sites: a queen core with zero drones and four sites with 40 drones each. Five additional dormant sites per participant have no owner, drones, or specialists. All sites begin as factories or generators, with empty shields and the supplied production and recharge clocks. There are no initial mines.

The square area is `playerCount * 10 * 10000 / 30`, preserving 30 sites per 100×100 units. Points occupy the full half-open square `[0, width) × [0, height)`, with no border padding or interior preference. Rejection sampling enforces five units of wrapped separation. Starts use randomized farthest-point selection across all sites. Minimum spacing, start separation, nearest claims, and expansion scoring all use the shortest toroidal distance, including across corners. Round-robin nearest-site claims allocate ten sites per start: the first five are owned. This prevents overlapping claims and balances ownership exactly.

The sampler evaluates at most 500 candidates. It minimizes the spread of neutral-site counts reached first from each player's nearest owned site, with the spread of mean distances to the three nearest neutral sites as a tie-breaker. It exits early when counts match and the distance spread is below 2% of map width. This estimates expansion opportunity; it doesn't simulate conquest orders, shields, or production. Focused scenarios cover seeds 0, 1, 73, and 4294967295 with every player count from 2 through 10. These scenarios allow at most four sites of wrapped expansion spread and require at least 20 units between cores; there is no universal fairness guarantee for every seed.

A shuffled ten-card industry deck contains 3–6 generators, selected per chart. A separate shuffled shield deck contains three 10-strength and seven 20-strength cards. Each participant receives the same decks in territorial order. Starting type/shield combinations, core attributes, and totals match exactly between participants. Shield strength doesn't depend on industry type. Quantized deck counts approximate the requested probabilities and keep whole-chart generator ratios within 30–60%.

## Evidence and limits

The [official rulebook](https://subterfuge-game.com/rulebook/) establishes five starting outposts, 160 drillers, and no initial mines.

The [community generation page](https://subterfuge.fandom.com/wiki/Map_and_Content_Generation) attributes a developer explanation describing ten sites per player, repelling points, nearest-five ownership, shared industry/shield decks, and selection among 500 candidates by expansion balance. It reports 30–60% generators and approximately one-third weaker shields. This attribution is partly unverified: search indexing exposed the text, while direct access was blocked. It isn't an official algorithm specification.

This implementation approximates repulsion with rejection sampling and farthest-point selection. Claims are exclusive and allocated in turns; candidate scoring uses a geometric proxy. It doesn't reproduce the proprietary generator or its reported typical two-site expansion spread. Toroidal geometry removes artificial boundary disadvantages, but does not guarantee symmetric starts or equal travel times. The generator only emits new coordinates and bounds; world rules, travel, rendering, persistence compatibility, public views, and UI integration belong to the caller. Its result contains no seed or RNG state.

Run focused checks with `node --import tsx --test src/game/generated-world.test.ts`.
